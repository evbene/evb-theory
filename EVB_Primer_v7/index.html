    Entropy‑Vector Boundary Theory (EVB Theory)¶

    Version 7 — Primer¶

    The EVB Primer provides a concise, high‑clarity introduction to the core concepts of Entropy‑Vector Boundary Theory. It is designed for researchers, engineers, and physicists who want a fast, intuitive understanding of the EVB timing‑geometry framework without reading the full main document.

    EVB Theory describes the universe as a timing‑structured continuum where geometry, energy, and particle behavior emerge from stable timing relationships. The Primer focuses on the essential ideas needed to understand the EVB particle model, timing‑geometry fields, and boundary interactions.

    1. What EVB Theory Proposes¶

    EVB Theory proposes that:

    • The universe is governed by timing‑geometry, not spatial geometry alone.
    • Particles are timing‑stable structures, not point objects.
    • Fields arise from timing‑vector interactions, not static forces.
    • Boundaries form where timing coherence changes, producing measurable physical effects.
    • Cosmological behavior emerges from large‑scale timing drift, not expansion of space.

    These principles unify particle behavior, field interactions, and cosmological dynamics under a single timing‑based framework.

    2. Timing‑Geometry Basics¶

    EVB Theory replaces traditional spatial primacy with timing primacy.

    2.1 Timing Cycles¶

    Every stable physical structure is built from repeating timing cycles.
    A timing cycle is defined by:

    • a stable period
    • a stable phase
    • a stable coherence relationship with neighboring cycles

    When timing cycles align, stable structures form.
    When they misalign, boundaries appear.

    2.2 Timing Vectors¶

    A timing vector represents:

    • the direction of timing drift
    • the magnitude of timing change
    • the coherence relationship between cycles

    Timing vectors interact to produce:

    • fields
    • forces
    • particle motion
    • boundary formation

    2.3 Timing Coherence¶

    Coherence determines whether timing cycles reinforce or interfere.

    High coherence → stable particles
    Low coherence → boundaries, fields, radiation

    3. EVB Particle Interpretation¶

    EVB Theory describes particles as timing‑stable bundles, not point objects.

    3.1 Particle Stability¶

    A particle exists when:

    • timing cycles lock into a stable configuration
    • timing vectors align
    • coherence remains above the stability threshold

    3.2 Particle Identity¶

    Particle type is determined by:

    • timing cycle count
    • timing vector orientation
    • coherence pattern
    • boundary geometry

    This allows EVB Theory to reinterpret:

    • electrons
    • protons
    • neutrons
    • neutrinos
    • photons

    as timing‑geometry structures rather than discrete objects.

    3.3 Predictive Behavior¶

    Particle interactions follow from:

    • timing vector addition
    • coherence changes
    • boundary formation
    • timing drift

    This produces a predictive framework for particle behavior without requiring point‑particle assumptions.

    4. Boundary Dynamics¶

    Boundaries form wherever timing coherence changes.

    4.1 Boundary Types¶

    EVB Theory identifies several boundary classes:

    • Coherence boundaries — where timing alignment shifts
    • Drift boundaries — where timing vectors diverge
    • Phase boundaries — where timing cycles slip
    • Energy boundaries — where timing amplification occurs

    4.2 Boundary Effects¶

    Boundaries produce:

    • measurable forces
    • field interactions
    • radiation
    • particle transitions
    • geometric distortions

    Boundaries are not “surfaces” — they are timing‑geometry transitions.

    5. Field Interpretation¶

    Fields arise from timing‑vector interactions.

    5.1 Electric Fields¶

    Electric fields represent timing‑vector divergence.

    5.2 Magnetic Fields¶

    Magnetic fields represent timing‑vector rotation.

    5.3 Gravitational Effects¶

    Gravitational behavior emerges from large‑scale timing drift, not curvature of spacetime.

    5.4 Radiation¶

    Radiation occurs when timing coherence drops below the stability threshold.

    6. Cosmological Implications¶

    EVB Theory proposes that:

    • The universe is not expanding; timing drift creates the appearance of expansion.
    • Redshift is a timing‑geometry effect, not Doppler motion.
    • Large‑scale structure emerges from timing‑vector alignment across regions.
    • Cosmological boundaries produce observable phenomena such as voids and filaments.

    This provides a timing‑based alternative to expansion‑based cosmology.

    7. Quick Reference Summary¶

    • Particles = timing‑stable bundles
    • Fields = timing‑vector interactions
    • Boundaries = coherence transitions
    • Radiation = coherence loss
    • Cosmology = large‑scale timing drift
    • Forces = timing‑vector geometry
    • Stability = coherence threshold
    • Motion = timing‑vector addition

    8. Purpose of the Primer¶

    The Primer is designed to:

    • introduce EVB Theory quickly
    • provide intuitive explanations
    • prepare readers for the full main document
    • establish the core timing‑geometry vocabulary
    • unify particle, field, and cosmological concepts under a single framework

    For full details, see EVB_Main_v7.md.
