## 2. Timing‑Geometry Basics

EVB Theory replaces traditional spatial primacy with **timing primacy**.

### 2.1 Timing Cycles  
Every stable physical structure is built from repeating timing cycles.  
A timing cycle is defined by:

- a stable period  
- a stable phase  
- a stable coherence relationship with neighboring cycles

When timing cycles align, stable structures form.  
When they misalign, boundaries appear.

### 2.2 Timing Vectors  
A timing vector represents:

- the direction of timing drift  
- the magnitude of timing change  
- the coherence relationship between cycles

Timing vectors interact to produce:

- fields  
- forces  
- particle motion  
- boundary formation

### 2.3 Timing Coherence  
Coherence determines whether timing cycles reinforce or interfere.

High coherence → stable particles  
Low coherence → boundaries, fields, radiation
