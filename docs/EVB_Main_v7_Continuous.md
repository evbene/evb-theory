EVB claims that every region of the continuum, from the entire cosmic domain, through biological boundary systems such as cellular membranes, and down to sub atomic regions spanning roughly 60 orders of magnitude, has a dynamic physical boundary. EVB shows that this boundary behavior is the single cross scale mechanism behind quantum decoherence, gravitational collapse, constant drift, structure formation, and the breakdown of cosmological predictions.

Modern physics has no existing theories that describe these dynamic boundaries across scales. EVB does.

## Abstract

### Background

Modern physics uses separate frameworks to explain quantum behavior, biological organization, gravitational collapse, and cosmological evolution. These domains rely on unrelated mechanisms and lack a unified description of cross scale phenomena. EVB Theory identifies this gap as a missing continuum level process.

### Mechanism

EVB proposes that the continuum contains dynamic boundary zones at every scale. These zones remain in persistent non equilibrium because collapse, coherence, and entropy compete within them and no process can reach completion. EVB models how boundary cycles, repeating sequences of partial collapse, partial coherence, and partial entropy release, regulate coherence, collapse, drift, and structure formation through timing based interactions.

### Results

The theory shows how boundary timing and boundary cycling produce behaviors currently treated as unrelated. Decoherence, gravitational collapse, biological boundary organization, constant drift, and failures in cosmological prediction arise from the same underlying continuum process. EVB provides a mathematical and conceptual framework for how boundary instability propagates through systems.

### Implications

EVB reframes multiple long standing anomalies as manifestations of a single mechanism and offers new predictions for cosmological evolution, biological boundary behavior, and sub atomic dynamics. The theory provides falsifiable predictions for boundary timing behavior in cosmological, biological, and sub atomic systems. EVB establishes boundary cycles as a fundamental driver of physical organization and offers a cross domain framework absent from existing physics.
