### Addendum AA  
Glossary of Core EVB Terms

Attraction  
Reinforcing timing comparison between boundaries.  
Attraction occurs when timing‑cycles align strongly enough to create reinforcement pockets that pull timing relocation toward the region of alignment.  

Boundary  
A region where timing‑cycles repeat reliably enough to maintain coherence.  
Boundaries exist because timing‑skins preserve identity and reinforcement pockets stabilize timing.  

Collapse  
Timing reorganization.  
Collapse occurs when drift exceeds correction capacity.  
Timing‑cycles fragment and reorganize into new patterns.  
Collapse is structural transformation, not destruction.  

Collapse Propagation  
The spread of timing reorganization through a boundary or across boundaries.  
Propagation speed depends on alignment strength, reinforcement depth, and manifold structure.  

Collapse Threshold  
The point where drift grows faster than the timing‑skin can correct it.  
Collapse thresholds determine when timing‑cycles break and reorganize.  

Communication  
Timing‑cycle transfer across boundaries without merging them.  
Communication occurs when timing comparison is strong enough to transmit timing information but weak enough to preserve boundary identity.  

Continuum Timing  
Timing without reinforcement.  
The continuum is diffuse timing where cycles do not repeat reliably.  
Boundaries emerge when local timing alignment becomes strong enough to form a timing‑skin.  

Coherence  
Alignment of timing‑cycles across a boundary or across layers.  
Coherence preserves identity, stability, and interaction.  
Loss of coherence increases drift and entropy.  

Desynchronization  
Drift across boundaries.  
Desynchronization weakens reinforcement, destabilizes interaction, and increases collapse likelihood.  

Divergence  
Conflicting timing comparison between boundaries.  
Divergence increases drift and pushes timing‑cycles apart.  
Divergence is the structural basis of repulsion.  

Drift  
A shift in timing alignment caused by gradients or interactions.  
Drift weakens coherence, lowers stability, and pushes timing‑cycles toward collapse thresholds.  

Emergence  
Formation of new timing‑cycles supported by lower‑layer reinforcement.  
Emergence creates new boundaries, new manifolds, and new layers of complexity.  

Entropy  
Timing dispersion.  
Entropy increases when timing‑cycles drift, reinforcement weakens, timing‑skins decay, or continuum gradients destabilize boundaries.  

Field  
Timing influence across distance.  
A field is a coherent timing‑gradient pattern that extends beyond a boundary.  
Field strength is gradient strength.  

Gradient  
A difference in timing alignment across a region.  
Gradients drive drift, motion, interaction, and collapse.  

Identity  
Coherence across timing‑cycles.  
Identity persists when timing‑cycles remain aligned.  
Identity weakens when timing‑cycles drift.  
Identity reorganizes when timing‑cycles collapse.  

Interaction  
Timing comparison across boundaries.  
Attraction is reinforcement.  
Repulsion is divergence.  
Bonding is shared reinforcement pockets.  
Communication is timing‑cycle transfer.  

Manifold  
A multi‑layer timing structure that connects timing‑cycles across scales.  
Manifolds preserve coherence, propagate timing, stabilize identity, and support emergence.  

Motion  
Timing relocation.  
Motion occurs when timing‑cycles shift toward one side of a boundary.  
Acceleration is gradient compression.  
Deceleration is gradient relaxation.  

Reinforcement Pocket  
A region of strong timing alignment that stabilizes timing‑cycles.  
Reinforcement pockets absorb drift, raise collapse thresholds, and support higher‑layer timing‑cycles.  

Repulsion  
Divergence in timing comparison.  
Repulsion occurs when timing‑cycles oppose each other strongly enough to increase drift and push boundaries apart.  

Synchronization  
Strong timing alignment across boundaries.  
Synchronization enables stable communication, coordinated behavior, and smooth propagation of timing changes.  

Timing  
The fundamental behavior of reality.  
Timing is structural repetition, alignment, drift, reinforcement, collapse, and reorganization.  

Timing‑Cycle  
A repeating pattern of timing alignment.  
Timing‑cycles preserve coherence inside boundaries and determine identity, stability, motion, and interaction.  

Timing‑Skin  
The surface where internal timing organization meets external timing variation.  
The timing‑skin preserves identity, corrects drift, compares timing, and translates timing shifts into motion.
